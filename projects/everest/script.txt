What happens to your body on Everest?
At the top, each breath holds only a third of the oxygen you get at sea level.
Your heart pounds, your head throbs, and even a few steps leave you gasping.
Your brain and lungs can start to swell with fluid, and frostbite can freeze your fingers in minutes.
That's why climbers call it the death zone, and most of them carry bottled oxygen to survive.
