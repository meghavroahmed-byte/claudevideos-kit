Why do your ears pop on a plane?
Behind each ear drum is a tiny pocket of air.
As the plane climbs, the cabin air pressure drops. But the air behind your ear drum stays the same, so your ear drum bulges outward.
A thin tube connects that pocket to the back of your throat. When you swallow or yawn, it opens and lets air through.
Pop! The pressure evens out, and you can hear clearly again.
